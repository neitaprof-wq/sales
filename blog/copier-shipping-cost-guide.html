How Much Does It Cost to Ship a Copier? Pricing Factors Explained

Copier shipping quotes can range from a couple hundred dollars to well over a thousand — here's exactly what drives the price, and how to get an accurate number the first time you ask.

"How much will it cost to ship my copier?" is the first question almost every customer asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on a handful of specific, knowable factors. Once you understand what actually moves the price, you can get an accurate quote in one call instead of guessing — and avoid surprise charges once the truck shows up.

The Five Factors That Determine Price

Every copier shipping quote comes down to some combination of these variables:

  • Weight and size — a 150-lb desktop copier ships very differently than an 800-lb production printer
  • Distance — a cross-town move and a cross-country or international move are priced on completely different scales
  • Crating and packaging needs — factory-style crating costs more than blanket-wrap but is often required for high-value or lease-return units
  • Pickup and delivery access — a loading dock is cheaper to work with than a walk-up office requiring liftgate and inside delivery
  • Service level and timing — standard freight scheduling costs less than expedited or date-guaranteed delivery

Why Weight and Dimensions Matter So Much

Freight carriers price primarily by weight and dimensional size, not just distance. A compact desktop copier at 150 lbs and a production-class machine at 800+ lbs are handled completely differently — the larger unit typically needs a pallet, banding, and sometimes a two-person lift team, all of which factor into the price. Always have the make/model or a shipping weight on hand when requesting a quote; "it's a big copier" isn't enough for an accurate number.

Crating: When It's Worth the Extra Cost

Not every copier needs a full wooden crate, but some situations call for it:

  • Quarterly lease returns, where the leasing company inspects for shipping damage and may bill you for it
  • International shipments, where crating protects against longer transit times and multiple handling points
  • High-value production printers with exposed panels, finishers, or glass components
  • Long-haul moves where the machine changes trucks more than once

For a short local move of a durable desktop unit, heavy-duty blanket wrap and pallet securing is often sufficient and keeps costs down. We'll tell you honestly which level of protection your shipment actually needs — not upsell you into unnecessary crating.

Pickup and Delivery Access Changes the Price

Two identical copiers going the identical distance can still cost differently based on access:

  • Dock-to-dock shipments (both locations have a loading dock) are the least expensive to handle
  • Liftgate service is needed when either end lacks a dock — common for small offices and home-based businesses
  • Inside delivery (placing the machine in a specific room, not just curbside) adds labor time
  • Limited-access locations — narrow doorways, stairs, elevators, or tight urban streets — may require additional equipment or manpower

Mentioning your access situation upfront (loading dock? stairs? elevator?) when requesting a quote is the single easiest way to get an accurate price the first time.

Standard vs. Expedited Timing

Standard freight scheduling — where your shipment moves within a normal multi-day transit window — is the most cost-effective option. If you're working against a hard deadline (a lease-return cutoff, an office move-in date, a sale closing), expedited or date-guaranteed service is available but priced at a premium since it requires dedicated routing.

How to Get an Accurate Quote in One Call

To skip the back-and-forth, have this information ready when you call or submit a quote request:

  1. Make and model (or at least approximate weight and dimensions)
  2. Pickup and delivery ZIP codes (or country, for international)
  3. Dock access at both ends — yes, no, or unknown
  4. Whether this is a one-time move or a recurring lease return
  5. Your target timeline

With those five details, we can give you a firm, no-obligation price — not a rough estimate that changes once the truck arrives.
