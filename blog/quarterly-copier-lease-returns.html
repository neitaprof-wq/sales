Copier Lease Return Shipping: The Complete Quarterly & End-of-Lease Guide

How to get leased copiers back on time and in inspection-ready condition, whether you are returning one machine or a quarterly batch from several offices.

Updated November 1, 2026 · By the USA Copier Movers team

To return a leased copier, get the return authorization number and return address from your leasing company, record the meter reading, remove toner and loose parts, photograph the machine, and book liftgate or inside pickup at least 1 to 2 weeks before your deadline. If your lease requires the hard drive to be cleared, your dealer or IT team handles that before pickup. For dealers and multi-site businesses with units coming due every quarter, a standing pickup schedule keeps every return on time and documented.

This guide covers copier lease return shipping from start to finish: return windows, what inspectors look for, packaging, and how to set up recurring quarterly pickups.

What Is a Quarterly Copier Lease Return?

Many businesses and copier dealers lease multifunction printers and production copiers on rolling terms, so a batch of machines comes due for return, upgrade, or redeployment every quarter. A quarterly lease return program bundles those pickups on a predictable schedule, usually tied to fiscal quarter-end. The leasing company gets its equipment back on time, and dealers can plan warehouse space and refurbishment work ahead of time instead of reacting to each machine.

What Happens If a Copier Lease Return Is Late?

Most lease agreements set a return window. Miss it and you can face continued rental charges, restocking fees, or an automatic extension you never meant to sign up for. A planned schedule gives you time to:

  • Confirm the exact return address and any paperwork or asset tags the leasing company requires
  • Book pickup during a quiet window for the office or warehouse
  • Document the machine's condition before it leaves your building
  • Avoid rush freight pricing that comes with last-minute bookings

How Do I Return a Leased Copier? Step by Step

  1. Get your return instructions. Ask the leasing company for the return address, return authorization (RA) number, and any required asset tags or forms. Put the RA number on every shipment.
  2. Record the meter reading. Print or photograph the usage report from the control panel on pickup day.
  3. Handle data removal on your side. Many leases require a hard-drive overwrite before return. Your dealer, leasing company, or IT provider runs it; keep the certificate if one is issued. This is not part of our service.
  4. Remove consumables and loose parts. Take out or secure toner, waste toner bottles, and paper, and tape doors and trays shut. Our copier prep checklist covers every step.
  5. Photograph the machine. All four sides, the control panel, and the meter display.
  6. Book liftgate or inside pickup. Most offices have no dock. Tell us the floor, elevator, and any stairs so the right crew and truck show up.
  7. Keep the delivery receipt. The signed receipt from the return location is your proof the unit came back on time.

When Should I Start Planning a Copier Lease Return?

Work backward from the return deadline. A simple timeline that keeps most returns on schedule:

  • 60 to 90 days out: Read the end-of-term section of your lease. Note the notice period, the return address, who pays freight, and any packaging or data requirements. Decide whether you are returning, upgrading, or buying out the machine.
  • 30 days out: Request the return authorization and asset tags from the leasing company. If a hard-drive overwrite is required, schedule it with your dealer or IT provider now so it is done well before pickup.
  • 2 to 4 weeks out: Request a shipping quote with pickup and delivery ZIP codes, dock access, and the make and model of each unit. Book the pickup date. Larger multi-site batches need the longer end of this window.
  • The week of pickup: Pull the meter reading, remove consumables, photograph each machine, and confirm building access, elevator reservations, and an on-site contact.
  • After delivery: File the signed delivery receipt with the RA number and your photos, and confirm with the leasing company that the unit was received.

If your lease ends at the same time as an office move, sort out which machines are going back and which are moving with you before booking. Our office move checklist for copiers and printers helps with that split.

What Do Leasing Companies Inspect on a Returned Copier?

Before a copier ships back, do your own walkaround using the same checkpoints the leasing company's inspector will use at the warehouse:

  • Exterior condition: scratches, cracked panels, missing covers or trim
  • Feed and finishing parts: document feeder rollers, staplers, and finishers working correctly
  • Consumables: toner, drums, and fuser at a reasonable wear level for the meter count
  • Meter reading: recorded and photographed at pickup, matching what was reported to the leasing company
  • Accessories: trays, cassettes, cables, and add-on finishers accounted for and shipped with the unit
Tip: Photograph each machine, all four sides plus the control panel and meter display, right before pickup. If a damage claim comes back weeks later, timestamped photos are the fastest way to settle it.

How Should a Lease Return Copier Be Packaged for Shipping?

Lease-return copiers are inspected on arrival, so shipping damage can turn into a billable claim against your account. A proper lease-return shipment includes:

  • Heavy-duty pallet wrapping or crating matched to the machine's size and weight
  • Feeders, trays, and finishers secured, or removed and boxed separately following manufacturer guidance
  • Corner and edge protection on every exposed panel
  • Liftgate pickup for units without dock access, so the machine is never dropped or tipped during loading

Who Pays for Copier Lease Return Shipping?

Your lease decides. Many agreements make the customer responsible for return freight to the leasing company's designated location, while some dealers arrange returns as part of an upgrade. Read the end-of-term section of your agreement, then get a firm quote early so the freight cost is not a surprise at quarter-end.

Copier Lease Return FAQ

Who pays to ship a leased copier back?

It depends on your lease. Many leases make the customer responsible for return freight to the leasing company's designated address. Check the end-of-term section of your agreement before the return window opens.

How far ahead should I book a copier lease return pickup?

Book at least 1 to 2 weeks before your return deadline. For multi-unit or multi-location quarterly batches, 3 to 4 weeks gives time to consolidate pickups and keep costs down.

Do I need to crate a copier for a lease return?

Not always. Many returns ship stretch-wrapped and padded on a pallet. Crating is recommended for long-haul, international, or high-value production units, or whenever the leasing company requires it.

Do you wipe the copier hard drive before a lease return?

No. Data wiping is not a service we provide. If your lease or security policy requires the drive to be cleared, have your dealer, leasing company, or IT provider run the overwrite and issue any certificate before pickup day.

Can you pick up leased copiers from several offices at once?

Yes. We consolidate multi-location pickups onto one schedule and one invoice.
