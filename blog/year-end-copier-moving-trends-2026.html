Trend 1: Year-End Copier Lease Returns

Many copier leases run 36 to 60 months and are timed to end at the end of a quarter or calendar year. That means a wave of off-lease copiers needs to go back to the leasing company or dealer in December and January. Most lease agreements spell out where the copier must be returned, how it must be packed and when, and late returns can mean extra monthly payments.

What to do now:

  • Read the return clause in your lease and find the return address and deadline.
  • Ask the leasing company whether the machine must be palletized or can be blanket-wrapped.
  • Book copier lease return shipping early. A liftgate pickup is usually needed, since most offices have no loading dock.
  • Get a bill of lading and a delivery receipt so you can prove the return.

Trend 2: Smaller Offices, Smaller Fleets

Hybrid work has changed how much businesses print. Many offices are trading big A3 floor copiers for fewer machines or compact A4 MFPs, and some are consolidating several floors into one. That creates two jobs at once: copier removal of the old machines and copier delivery of the new ones. When the old copier is leased, it goes back to the lessor. When it is owned, it may go to a dealer for trade-in, a remarketer or a recycler.

A copier moving company can handle both in one trip: pick up the old copy machine, deliver and place the new one, and take the old unit wherever it needs to go.

Trend 3: Office Moves in Q4 and Q1

Commercial leases often renew at the turn of the year, so office moves pile up in December and January. Do businesses switch copiers when moving offices? Many do, because an office move is the easiest time to end a lease and bring in a new machine. Others move their existing copier. Either way, the copier should not ride with the furniture. It needs to stay upright, be locked and wrapped, and be moved with a stair crawler or ramp, not carried. See our office move copier checklist and how to move a copier guide.

Trend 4: Dealers Clearing Inventory Before Year-End

Copier dealers and office equipment companies push to deliver machines before December 31 and clear out trade-ins and refurbished units. That means more dealer-to-customer copier deliveries, more inside delivery and placement, and more machines that need somewhere to wait. If your warehouse is full, copier storage can hold machines until the customer is ready, and we can deliver them from storage when you call.

Trend 5: Holiday Freight Costs and Tight Schedules

Freight gets busier between Thanksgiving and New Year's, and holidays shorten the number of pickup and delivery days. Fuel prices also move copier shipping costs up and down through the year. How Much Does It Cost to Ship a Copier in 2026?

There is no single price, because copier shipping cost depends on:

  • Distance: a local copier move across town costs less than long-distance copier shipping across states.
  • Size and weight: a desktop printer under 100 lbs is very different from a 300 to 400 lb office copier or a production machine.
  • Packing: blanket wrap or palletized and stretch-wrapped.
  • Services: liftgate, inside delivery, stairs, elevators, placement and debris removal.
  • Timing: rush and holiday-week deliveries cost more.

Copier freight class for LTL shipping is usually set by density, which is the weight compared to the size of the packed shipment, so accurate dimensions and weight give you an accurate quote.

How to Prepare a Copier for Shipping

  1. Print a configuration page and note the meter reading for your lease records.
  2. Lock the scanner and carriage if the model has a transport lock.
  3. Remove toner, the waste toner box and paper so nothing spills.
  4. Remove or secure finishers and paper decks and tape doors closed.
  5. Keep it upright. A copy machine should never be laid down for transport.
  6. Use a copier moving dolly or stair crawler, and load it on a liftgate truck.
