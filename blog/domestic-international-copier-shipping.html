Export Copier Shipping: Domestic vs. International Guide

Shipping a copier across the country and shipping one overseas are not the same job. Here's what changes — crating, paperwork, timelines, and cost — for Canon, Xerox, Ricoh, Konica Minolta, Sharp, Kyocera, Toshiba, HP, and Lexmark equipment.

USA Copier Movers ships copiers and office equipment both domestically, within the United States, and internationally, to destinations overseas. The two types of shipments share the same starting point — a properly packed machine — but diverge quickly after that. Knowing the difference helps you plan realistic timelines and avoid surprises.

Domestic Copier Shipping

A domestic shipment moves by truck freight — dedicated, LTL (less-than-truckload), or liftgate delivery — door to door within the U.S. There's no customs process, no ocean or air leg, and delivery windows are measured in days, not weeks. Domestic shipping is the right fit for office relocations, dealer inventory transfers, and quarterly lease returns within the country.

International Copier Shipping

An international shipment adds several extra steps on top of everything a domestic move requires:

  • Export-grade crating — full wooden crating rather than pallet wrap, built to survive ocean or air handling and, where required, heat-treated/ISPM-15 compliant for customs
  • Freight forwarding — coordination with a freight forwarder to book ocean or air cargo space and manage the international leg
  • Customs documentation — commercial invoice, packing list, and any export/import paperwork the destination country requires
  • Longer timelines — ocean freight commonly runs several weeks port-to-port, plus customs clearance time on both ends
  • Destination handling — many destinations require a local customs broker or agent to clear and deliver the equipment on arrival

Exporting a Copier Overseas: What You'll Need

  • Export crate — international shipments should be fully crated. Wood packaging must meet ISPM-15 (heat-treated and stamped) for most countries. See our copier prep checklist for what to do before crating.
  • Commercial invoice & packing list — make, model, serial number, value, weight, and dimensions for each unit.
  • Export filing — U.S. exports valued over $2,500 per commodity classification (or needing an export license) require an Electronic Export Information (EEI) filing through AESDirect; your freight forwarder typically handles this.
  • Destination requirements — some countries restrict or add duties on used office equipment. Confirm import rules with the consignee before booking.
  • Air vs. ocean — air is fastest for a single urgent unit; ocean (LCL or full container) is usually most cost-effective for multiple machines.
