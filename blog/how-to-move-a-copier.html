How to Move a Copier Safely: Step-by-Step Guide for Office Moves

Down the hall, across town, or across the country: here is how to move a copier without damaging the machine, running afoul of your lease, or losing a day of work.

Updated November 1, 2026 · By the USA Copier Movers team

To move a copier safely, keep it upright, take out the toner and loose parts, lock the scanner if your model has one, remove trays and finishers, and move it with a liftgate truck, a heavy-duty dolly, and a crew that handles copiers regularly. Before moving day, check your lease for an equipment-location clause, and if the hard drive needs to be cleared, have your dealer or IT team do that before pickup. For anything beyond rolling the machine across the same room, hire professional copier movers.

Can I Move a Copier Myself, or Do I Need Copier Movers?

Two people can usually carry a small desktop printer or MFP. A floor-standing office copier is a different job, and most copier damage happens when someone treats it like a filing cabinet. The main reasons to call a copier moving company:

  • Weight. Floor-standing office copiers commonly weigh about 150 to 400+ lbs, and production printers can go well past 500 lbs.
  • Top-heavy shape. Copiers are tall with a high center of gravity. Thresholds, ramps, curbs, and elevator lips are where they tip, and a tipping copier can injure whoever is holding it.
  • Casters are for short moves. The built-in wheels are made for sliding the machine a few feet on a smooth floor, not for parking lots or truck ramps.
  • Your lease and service contract. Damage from a do-it-yourself move is usually your responsibility, and some service agreements exclude it.

Rolling a copier a few feet within the same room is fine. Stairs, elevators, a truck, or a second building means it is time for professional copier transport.

What Should I Check in My Copier Lease Before Moving It?

Pull the paperwork before you book anything. If the machine is leased:

  • Look for an equipment-location clause. Many leases require written notice before the copier moves to a new address.
  • Give your dealer or service provider the new address so meter reads, toner shipments, and service calls follow the machine.
  • Ask whether the dealer has any requirements for how the move is handled or documented.

How Do I Plan the Route at Both Buildings?

Most copier moving delays happen at the building, not on the highway. Walk the path the machine will travel at both the pickup and the delivery address and write down:

  • Doorway and hallway widths, measured against the copier's width and depth with any finisher attached
  • Stairs: how many flights, and whether there is a landing to turn on
  • Elevator door width, interior depth, and weight capacity
  • Whether there is a loading dock, or whether a liftgate truck is needed
  • Building rules: certificate of insurance requests, move-in hours, and freight elevator reservations
  • The exact spot the copier will go at the new site, with an outlet and network drop nearby

Include these details when you request a quote. Stairs, missing docks, and tight turns change the crew and equipment needed, and knowing about them upfront keeps the price firm. If either building has stairs or narrow halls, read our guide to white glove copier delivery for tight offices.

Who Wipes the Copier Hard Drive Before a Move?

You do, through your dealer, leasing company, or IT provider. Most multifunction copiers store images of scanned, copied, and faxed documents on an internal drive, and clearing it is an IT and security task that happens before the movers arrive. USA Copier Movers does not wipe, erase, or remove copier hard drives.

What to do on your side:

  • Ask your dealer or IT provider to run the manufacturer's data-overwrite or security purge function.
  • If you are in a regulated field such as healthcare, legal, or finance, ask them for a record or certificate of the wipe.
  • If the copier is just moving to your new office and staying with you, a wipe may not be required, but confirm with your IT team who has access to the machine while it is in transit.

How Do I Prepare a Copier for Moving?

Ten minutes of prep prevents most in-transit damage:

  1. Print a configuration page so you have the IP address, network settings, and address book for setup later.
  2. Remove toner cartridges and seal them in bags. Loose toner is the biggest mess risk.
  3. Take out staple cartridges and empty the paper trays.
  4. Engage the scanner transport lock if your model has one.
  5. Detach finishers, output trays, and add-on cabinets that are designed to come off.
  6. Shut down through the normal power-off sequence, unplug, and coil the cables.
  7. Photograph all four sides, the control panel, and the serial number tag.

How Do Professional Movers Transport a Copier?

This is the part you are paying a copier mover for. A proper copier move includes:

  • Upright handling from start to finish. The machine never goes on its side or back.
  • The right equipment. Heavy-duty or stair-climbing dollies, ramps, straps, and a liftgate truck rather than a standard hand truck.
  • Protective packaging. Blanket wrap and padding for local moves; palletizing or crating for long-distance freight.
  • Load securing. Strapped to the truck wall or a pallet so it cannot roll or tip under braking.
  • Building protection. Floor runners and door-jamb padding at both addresses.
  • Inside placement. The copier set where you want it, not left at the curb.

How Do I Set Up a Copier After Moving It?

Once the machine is in place, give it time to acclimate before powering on, especially if it rode in a cold or humid truck, because condensation can form inside. A common rule of thumb is to wait a few hours. Then:

  • Reinstall toner and consumables.
  • Release the scanner lock before powering on, since starting up with it engaged can throw errors.
  • Reconnect the network cable and check the IP settings against your configuration page.
  • Run a test copy, print, and scan, and call your dealer if any error codes appear.

Moving a Copier to Another Floor, Another Building, or Another State

Same building, different floor

Usually a freight elevator job. The risk points are the elevator threshold and tight corners, so plan on at least two people and a proper dolly. If stairs are involved and there is no elevator, bring in a professional crew.

Across town

A local copier relocation needs a liftgate truck, straps, and blanket wrap. It is often finished in a single morning, with the machine back in service the same day.

Out of state or cross-country

Long-distance copier moves travel as freight. The machine is typically palletized or crated, then moved by LTL freight or a dedicated truck, with liftgate and inside delivery at the destination. Weight, distance, and service level drive the price; see how much it costs to ship a copier.

Copier Moving FAQ

How much does a copier weigh?

It depends on the class of machine. Desktop multifunction printers are often under 100 lbs, floor-standing office copiers commonly weigh about 150 to 400+ lbs, and production printers can go well past 500 lbs. The spec sheet for your model lists the exact weight.

Can a copier be moved on its side?

No. A copier should stay upright the whole way. Laying it down can shift internal parts, knock the scanner out of alignment, and spill residual toner inside the machine.

How much does it cost to move a copier?

Local copier moves are priced mostly on labor, stairs, and equipment. Long-distance moves are priced on weight, distance, packaging, and the delivery service you need. Our copier shipping cost guide breaks down each factor, or request a free quote for a firm price.

Do I need to tell my leasing company before moving a copier?

Usually, yes. Many copier leases include an equipment-location clause that requires notice before the machine changes address. Check your agreement and call your leasing company or dealer before moving day.
