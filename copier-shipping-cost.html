“How much does it cost to ship a copier?” is the question we hear most, and the honest answer is that it depends on the machine and the job. Two moves of the same model can be priced very differently if one is a dock-to-dock transfer across town and the other is a cross-country shipment to a third-floor office with no elevator. Instead of publishing numbers that would not match your job, this guide walks through what actually drives the cost.

1. The Copier Itself

Size and weight are the starting point. A desktop MFP can be handled by one or two people, while a floor-standing production copier with a finisher and paper decks needs more crew and heavier equipment. The make and model tell us the dimensions and approximate weight, so include them in every quote request. Accessories such as finishers, large-capacity trays and cabinets add size and handling time.

2. Distance and Route

Local moves within one metro area are usually priced around crew time. Regional moves along a corridor like I-95 or I-64 and long-distance shipments are priced around the route and how the machine travels. Destinations far from major freight lanes can cost more to reach than a longer trip between two big cities.

3. How the Copier Travels

  • Dedicated truck: the copier rides on a truck assigned to your job, often the fastest option with the least handling
  • Shared freight: the copier is palletized and moves with other shipments, which can lower the cost on long routes but adds handling and transit time

Our nationwide copier freight shipping page explains these options in more detail.

4. Pickup and Delivery Access

Access is one of the biggest variables. A loading dock at both ends is the simplest case. Each of the following adds time or equipment:

  • No dock, which requires a liftgate truck
  • Inside delivery past the front door
  • Stairs, especially more than a few steps
  • Small elevators, long hallways or tight turns
  • Building rules such as reserved elevator time, after-hours windows or security check-in

5. Packaging and Preparation

Short moves often need only blanket wrapping. Long-distance freight usually requires the copier to be palletized and shrink-wrapped, and some high-value machines are crated. The more protective packaging the shipment needs, the more it adds to the job.

6. Timing

Flexible dates usually cost less than a fixed day and time. Rush pickups, guaranteed delivery appointments, weekend work and after-hours moves all take extra planning.

7. Number of Machines

Shipping several copiers on one trip usually lowers the cost per machine compared with separate trips. Dealers and companies handling multiple lease returns often consolidate for that reason.
